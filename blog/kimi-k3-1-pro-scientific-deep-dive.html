Abstract

Kimi K3.1 Pro represents a significant advancement in open-weight frontier intelligence, released October 23, 2026 by Moonshot AI. Built on the Kimi K3 foundation architecture (2.8 trillion total parameters, 104 billion active per token), K3.1 Pro achieves state-of-the-art performance through substantially scaled post-training reinforcement learning rather than architectural modifications. This analysis presents a comprehensive technical examination of both the underlying K3 architecture and the K3.1 Pro post-training methodology.

The K3 architecture introduces three fundamental innovations that enable efficient scaling to 2.8T parameters: Kimi Delta Attention (KDA), a linear attention mechanism with channel-wise gating that maintains fixed-size recurrent state independent of sequence length; Attention Residuals (AttnRes), specifically Block AttnRes, which replaces fixed residual connections with learned depth-wise attention over layer outputs; and Stable LatentMoE, a latent-space Mixture-of-Experts design that activates 16 of 896 routed experts per token plus 2 shared experts, achieving 1.78% expert activation while maintaining quality through compression to 3584-dimensional latent space.

The model employs a hybrid attention stack: 93 total layers organized as 69 KDA layers + 24 Gated Multi-head Latent Attention (MLA) layers interleaved in a 3:1 ratio (KDA×3 → MLA×1), with one additional MLA layer ensuring global attention at the final layer. The architecture uses NoPE (No Positional Embeddings) throughout — position information emerges entirely from KDA's decay dynamics and the occasional global MLA layers. Feed-forward networks use SiTU-GLU activation (Sigmoid-Tanh Unit with Gated Linear Unit) with softcapping parameters β₁=4 and β₂=25 to prevent activation explosion at depth. Native multimodal vision is provided by MoonViT-V2 (401M parameters), trained from scratch via next-token prediction.

K3.1 Pro's improvements stem from joint reinforcement learning across hundreds of downstream domains with substantially larger post-training compute than K3. Key results: Terminal-Bench 4.0 improved from 13% (K3) to 65.7% (K3.1 Pro), placing it ahead of GPT-6 Astra (58.2%) and narrowly behind Claude Opus 5.5 (66.4%); GDPval-AA increased 312 Elo to 1,846, ranking 3rd globally behind only Claude Opus 5.5 (1,866) and Muse Wonder Full (1,857); hallucination rate on stress tests dropped from 23.4% to 8.3%; and the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index score rose from 44 to 55, placing K3.1 Pro 4th globally with only a 3-point gap from 1st place.

Moonshot AI applied targeted cybersecurity controls during post-training, removing cyber-related data from both the mid-training corpus and RL environment mix. This reduced performance on xdev-bench-hard from 90.5% (uncontrolled checkpoint) to 81.0% (K3.1 Pro), while maintaining that K3.1 Pro's cyber capability remains "behind that of frontier proprietary models that have been publicly available for at least three months." Full model weights are scheduled for release by November 4, 2026, under Apache 2.0 license.

This document serves as the definitive technical reference for Kimi K3.1 Pro architecture, training methodology, benchmark performance, and deployment considerations. All claims are sourced from official Moonshot AI publications, the ArXiv technical paper (2607.24653), independent evaluations by Artificial Analysis, and verified against HuggingFace model documentation as of October 24, 2026.

1. Introduction & Model Overview

1.1 What is Kimi K3.1 Pro?

Kimi K3.1 Pro is a post-trained enhancement of the Kimi K3 base model, released October 23, 2026 by Moonshot AI alongside its faster variant, Kimi K3.1 Pro Highspeed. Unlike architectural updates that introduce new components or modify core designs, K3.1 Pro demonstrates the impact of substantially scaled reinforcement learning compute applied to an already-frontier architecture.

The model achieved immediate recognition in independent evaluations: Artificial Analysis raised its Intelligence Index score from 44 (Kimi K3) to 55 (K3.1 Pro), an 11-point gain that placed it 4th globally, ahead of GPT-6 Astra, Gemini 4 Argon, and Claude Fable 5.1. On GDPval-AA, which measures performance on real-world work tasks, K3.1 Pro achieved 1,846 Elo — a 312-point increase from K3 — ranking 3rd globally behind only Claude Opus 5.5 (1,866 Elo) and Muse Wonder Full (1,857 Elo).

Moonshot AI's official announcement describes K3.1 Pro as "our most capable 3T-class open-weight model to date, with a 1-million-token context window. It delivers substantial improvements across coding, 3D creation, financial research, and other agentic capabilities."

1.2 Relationship to Kimi K3

K3.1 Pro shares its entire architecture with Kimi K3, the 2.8-trillion-parameter base model released in July 2026 with full weights published July 27, 2026. The K3 architecture was documented in the technical paper "Kimi K3: Open Frontier Intelligence" (arXiv:2607.24653), which introduced three core innovations:

  • Kimi Delta Attention (KDA): A linear attention mechanism extending Gated DeltaNet with channel-wise forget gates, maintaining fixed-size recurrent state regardless of sequence length
  • Attention Residuals (AttnRes): Specifically Block AttnRes, replacing fixed unit-weight residual connections with learned depth-wise attention over layer outputs
  • Stable LatentMoE: Latent-space Mixture-of-Experts with 896 routed experts per layer, activating 16 per token (1.78% sparsity) plus 2 shared experts

The model employs a hybrid attention stack: 69 KDA layers + 24 Gated Multi-head Latent Attention (MLA) layers, organized in a 3:1 interleaving pattern (KDA×3 → MLA×1) with one additional MLA layer at depth 93 to ensure the final layer performs global attention. Total parameter count: 2.8T (2,784B text backbone + 401M MoonViT-V2 vision encoder), with 104B activated per token during inference.

K3.1 Pro preserves this architecture entirely. The improvements come from:

  1. Substantially larger post-training RL compute, with joint optimization across hundreds of downstream domains
  2. Infrastructure optimizations at every layer: RL framework, long-context inference engine, sandbox infrastructure, rollout scheduling, and environment quality checks
  3. Targeted data controls for cybersecurity: removal of cyber-related content from mid-training corpus and RL environments

This approach — scaling post-training compute rather than modifying architecture — yielded measurable gains across every evaluated domain. Moonshot AI states: "As a result, K3.1 Pro achieves consistent gains in every domain."

2. Architecture Overview

2.1 High-Level Design Philosophy

The Kimi K3 architecture (which K3.1 Pro inherits) was designed to scale information flow across three dimensions:

  1. Sequence Length — KDA linear attention maintains fixed recurrent state, enabling efficient processing of 1M-token contexts without quadratic memory growth
  2. Network Depth — Block AttnRes prevents hidden-state dilution by learning which layer outputs matter, replacing fixed unit-weight accumulation
  3. Model Width — Stable LatentMoE achieves extreme sparsity (1.78% expert activation) through latent-space compression, allowing 896 routed experts per layer while keeping active parameters at 104B

These innovations yielded approximately 2.5× improvement in overall scaling efficiency compared to Kimi K2, Moonshot AI's previous-generation model. The architecture paper states: "Together with Stable LatentMoE, which effectively activates 16 of 896 routed experts per token, and refined training and data recipes, these advances yield an approximately 2.5× improvement in overall scaling efficiency over Kimi K2."

2.3 Hybrid Attention Stack

K3 employs a 3:1 hybrid attention design:

  • 69 KDA layers — linear attention with channel-wise gating, O(1) memory per token
  • 24 Gated MLA layers — global full attention with low-rank KV compression
  • Pattern: KDA×3 → MLA×1, repeated 23 times, then one final MLA layer (23 × 4 + 1 = 93 total)

This design balances compute efficiency (KDA's linear cost) with modeling capacity (MLA's global receptive field). The periodic MLA layers provide "checkpoints" where the model can integrate information globally, while KDA layers handle the majority of computation with fixed memory overhead regardless of context length.

2.4 Layer Organization

Each transformer layer contains:

  1. Pre-LayerNorm
  2. Attention sublayer (KDA or Gated MLA)
  3. Attention Residual connection (Block AttnRes: learned weighted sum over block-level representations)
  4. Pre-LayerNorm
  5. MoE FFN sublayer (Stable LatentMoE with SiTU-GLU activation)
  6. FFN Residual connection (standard additive residual)

Block AttnRes groups layers into approximately 12 blocks (93 layers / ~8 layers per block ≈ 12 blocks, though exact blocking not specified in paper). Within each block, standard additive residuals; across blocks, depth-wise attention over block-level summary representations. This reduces the attention computation from O(L²) for Full AttnRes to O(B²) where B is the number of blocks, achieving most of Full AttnRes's gains with minimal overhead.

The architecture uses NoPE (No Positional Embeddings) throughout. There are no RoPE layers, no absolute position embeddings, no ALiBi. Position information emerges entirely from KDA's decay dynamics (the channel-wise forget gates create position-dependent behavior) and the periodic MLA layers' ability to attend globally.

2.5 Gated Multi-head Latent Attention

While KDA provides linear-complexity attention, some patterns benefit from full global attention. K3 uses Gated Multi-head Latent Attention (Gated MLA) for 24 of its 93 layers, providing a hybrid attention stack.

Multi-head Latent Attention

Traditional multi-head attention splits the hidden dimension across H heads:

Q, K, V ∈ ℝn×d
Split into H heads of dimension d/H each
Compute attention per head
Concatenate and project

MLA instead projects to a shared latent space before splitting into heads:

1. Project to latent: h → latent (compressed)
2. Split latent into heads
3. Compute attention
4. Concat and project back to h

This reduces the KV cache size significantly—instead of storing separate K, V for each head, we store a single compressed latent representation.

Gating Mechanism

The "Gated" in Gated MLA refers to an additional gating operation that modulates attention outputs:

attn_out = Attention(Q, K, V)
gated_out = attn_out ⊙ σ(Wgate · h)

Where σ is typically a sigmoid or similar activation. This gating helps prevent attention sink phenomena where the first token receives disproportionate attention weight.

2.6 Hybrid Attention Stack

K3's 93 layers follow a specific interleaving pattern:

[KDA × 3] → [Gated MLA × 1] → [KDA × 3] → [Gated MLA × 1] → ... → [Gated MLA × 1]

Layer Breakdown:

  • 69 KDA layers (~74% of layers)
  • 24 Gated MLA layers (~26% of layers)
  • 1 final dense layer

Design Rationale

This hybrid design balances efficiency and capability:

  1. KDA dominance (69 layers): Most layers use linear attention for efficient long-context processing
  2. Periodic MLA injections (every 4th layer): Global attention layers provide precise retrieval and global comparisons
  3. 3:1 ratio: Optimizes the tradeoff between context length capability and retrieval precision

The pattern ensures that:

  • Information flows efficiently through the linear KDA backbone
  • Global MLA layers periodically "reset" and provide precise cross-token comparisons
  • The model maintains O(n) average complexity while retaining global attention capability

2.7 No Positional Embeddings (NoPE)

One of K3's most unconventional design choices is NoPE — No Positional Embeddings. The model has zero explicit positional encoding—no RoPE, no absolute embeddings, no learned position encodings.

Why Remove Positional Embeddings?

Traditional positional embeddings create several problems for long-context models:

  • Fixed Context Limit: RoPE has a maximum position it was trained on; extrapolation beyond that degrades performance
  • Interpolation Artifacts: Extending context via position interpolation introduces distribution shift
  • Compute Overhead: RoPE requires rotation operations at every attention layer
  • KV Cache Constraints: Positional encodings complicate KV cache compression and reuse

How Does K3 Learn Position?

Without explicit position encodings, K3 learns positional patterns implicitly through:

  1. Causal Masking: Attention can only attend to previous positions, providing structural position information
  2. KDA's Recurrence: The recurrent formulation inherently encodes sequence order through state updates
  3. Learned Patterns: The model learns position-dependent patterns from data (e.g., "the" often appears early in sentences)
  4. Decay Mechanisms: KDA's decay gates provide implicit distance weighting

Benefits of NoPE

  • Unlimited Context: No hardcoded position limit; the 1M-token window is a deployment choice, not an architectural constraint
  • Better Generalization: Model generalizes to sequences longer than training distribution
  • Simpler Architecture: One fewer operation per layer
  • KV Cache Flexibility: Cached keys/values don't carry position-dependent encodings, enabling more aggressive compression
Tradeoff: NoPE requires the model to learn positional patterns from data, which may require more training compute. However, the benefits for long-context capability and generalization appear to outweigh this cost.

2.8 Feed-Forward Networks and Activation Functions

After each attention layer, K3 applies a feed-forward network (FFN) with the SiTU-GLU activation function.

SiTU-GLU Activation

SiTU-GLU is a gated activation combining SiLU (Sigmoid Linear Unit) with a gating mechanism:

SiLU(x) = x · σ(x)  where σ is sigmoid
GLU(x, Wg, Wup) = (Wup · x) ⊙ σ(Wg · x)

SiTU-GLU: applies SiLU to both branches

The FFN structure is:

gate = SiLU(Wgate · h)
up = SiLU(Wup · h)
ffn_out = Wdown · (gate ⊙ up)

Why SiTU-GLU?

  • Gating: Allows the network to dynamically control information flow
  • Non-linearity: SiLU provides smooth, non-monotonic activation
  • Better Gradients: Compared to ReLU, SiLU avoids dead neurons
  • Empirical Performance: GLU variants consistently outperform simple FFNs in large language models

2.9 KV Cache Mechanisms

For a 1-million-token context, KV cache management is critical. K3 uses several optimizations to make this practical.

Compressed KV Cache with MLA

Gated MLA's latent projection reduces KV cache size:

Standard Attention: Store K, V ∈ ℝn×d×h for all heads
MLA: Store compressed latent ∈ ℝn×dlatent

Cache size reduction: ~4-8× depending on compression ratio

KDA Recurrent State

KDA layers don't need to store past KV pairs—they maintain a fixed-size hidden state:

Standard Attention KV cache: O(n · d)
KDA hidden state: O(d²) — independent of sequence length

This is the key to K3's 1M-token capability: 69 of 93 layers use constant memory regardless of context length.

Eviction Strategies

For the 24 Gated MLA layers that do require KV caching, K3 likely uses selective eviction:

  • Sliding Window: Keep recent tokens
  • Landmark Tokens: Always keep first N tokens
  • Importance Sampling: Keep tokens with high attention scores
  • Compression: Quantize older tokens to lower precision
VestigeKV Research: A recent paper (VestigeKV: The NoPE-MLA KV Cache Carries Its Own Eviction Signal, arXiv:2609.03949) specifically studies KV cache compression for NoPE-MLA models like K3, showing that the cache carries its own eviction signal in a "vestigial branch" that can be used for smart compression without observing future attention patterns.

2.10 Vision Encoder: MoonViT-V2

K3 is a native multimodal model with vision capabilities built into the same architecture. The vision encoder is MoonViT-V2, a 401M-parameter vision transformer.

Architecture

  • Type: Vision Transformer (ViT)
  • Parameters: 401 million
  • Input: Images, video frames
  • Output: Visual tokens that are directly processed by the text backbone

Multimodal Integration

Unlike models that use a separate vision tower with a projection layer, K3's vision tokens are native first-class citizens:

3. Kimi Delta Attention (KDA)

3.1 Motivation & Background

Full attention scales quadratically with sequence length: O(n²) memory and compute. For 1M-token contexts, storing attention matrices for even a single layer would require terabytes of memory at FP16 precision (10¹² tokens² × 2 bytes). This quadratic bottleneck has driven research into efficient attention mechanisms that maintain modeling quality while reducing complexity.

Kimi Delta Attention extends a lineage of delta-rule-based linear attention mechanisms. The progression:

  1. DeltaNet: Applies delta-rule updates to an associative memory matrix, treating attention as a recurrent process
  2. Gated DeltaNet: Adds a scalar forget gate per head, enabling the model to decay old information
  3. Kimi Delta Attention: Extends Gated DeltaNet with channel-wise (per-dimension) forget gates, providing fine-grained control over which semantic dimensions persist versus decay

This progression increases expressivity: Gated DeltaNet can forget entire heads uniformly, but KDA can selectively retain some key dimensions while forgetting others within the same head. Research shows this finer-grained gating enables more effective use of the limited finite-state RNN memory that linear attention provides.

3.2 Mathematical Formulation

KDA maintains a recurrent state S ∈ ℝK×V (where K is key dimension, V is value dimension) that is updated at each position. For a given query q, key k, value v, and channel-wise forget gate β ∈ ℝK, the update follows:

S_t = diag(β) · S_{t-1} + δ · (k_t ⊗ v_t)
o_t = S_t^T q_t

Where:

  • diag(β) is a diagonal matrix with forget gates on the diagonal, enabling per-channel decay
  • δ is the delta-rule write coefficient (scalar), controlling how strongly the new key-value association is written
  • k_t ⊗ v_t is the outer product, forming a rank-1 update to the memory matrix
  • o_t is the output, retrieved by querying the current state with q_t

The channel-wise gating β ∈ [0, 1]K determines decay rates independently for each of the K key dimensions. A gate value near 1 means "remember this dimension," near 0 means "forget rapidly." This enables the model to learn which semantic features should persist across many tokens (long-range dependencies) versus which should decay quickly (local features).

3.3 Channel-Wise Gating Mechanism

The forget gates are computed as:

β = σ(W_β · x + b_β)

Where σ is the sigmoid function, ensuring β ∈ [0, 1], and x is the input representation at the current token. The gates are content-dependent: the model learns to adjust decay rates based on what it's currently processing.

Extended parameter ranges: The K3 paper mentions allowing gates in ℝ (not constrained to [0,1]) and delta coefficients δ in ℝ (not just positive values), following extensions proposed in analysis of KDA expressivity. These extensions enable the model to amplify (β > 1) or invert (δ < 0) associations, increasing the set of linear transformations KDA can approximate.

3.4 Recurrent State Dynamics

The key property: S has fixed size K×V regardless of sequence length. At 1M tokens, KDA still maintains the same memory footprint as at 1K tokens. This contrasts with full attention, where KV cache grows linearly with sequence length (storing K and V vectors for every past token).

Trade-off: Fixed state means information must be compressed. The model cannot perfectly recall arbitrary details from 500K tokens ago — it must learn which information to retain in the finite-capacity recurrent state. The channel-wise gates provide the mechanism: learn to preserve high-level semantic features (topics, entities, narrative structure) while letting low-level details (specific word choices, syntax from distant past) decay.

The periodic MLA layers compensate: every 4th layer performs global attention, allowing the model to reintegrate detailed information when needed. The 3:1 KDA:MLA ratio means 74% of layers use fixed-state linear attention, with 26% providing global "checkpoints."

3.5 Comparison to Full Attention

Property Full Attention KDA
Time Complexity (per token) O(n) O(K×V)
Memory (KV cache) O(n) — grows with sequence O(1) — fixed state
Information Capacity Unlimited (stores all past) Finite (K×V matrix)
Positional Encoding Required (RoPE, ALiBi, etc.) Not required (emergent from decay)
Long-Context Quality High (if trained properly) Depends on state capacity

At 1M-token context, KDA at a single layer with K=V=128 maintains a 128×128 = 16,384-element state, approximately 32 KB at FP16. Full attention at the same layer would require storing K and V vectors for 1M tokens: 1M × 128 × 2 (K and V) × 2 bytes = 512 MB. The 16,000× memory reduction is what makes million-token inference tractable.

3.6 Implementation Details

K3 uses 96 attention heads with 7168 total hidden dimension, yielding 7168/96 ≈ 74.67 dimensions per head (the paper likely uses head_dim=64 or 128 with some heads having different sizes, though exact per-head dimensions are not specified). For KDA layers, the key and value dimensions determine state size.

Algorithm-system co-design: Moonshot AI developed custom CUDA kernels for KDA that fuse the recurrent update and query operations, reducing memory traffic. The K3 infrastructure section mentions "algorithm–system co-design for KDA" as a key enabler of 2.8T-scale training.

The model ships with MXFP4 quantized weights, meaning the recurrent state parameters (W_β, projections for q/k/v/δ) are stored at 4-bit precision, with activations (the state S itself, gates β, outputs o) computed at MXFP8 (8-bit) precision during inference.

4. Attention Residuals (AttnRes)

4.1 The Depth Dilution Problem

Standard residual connections in transformers use fixed unit weights:

h_{l+1} = h_l + F_l(h_l)

Where F_l is the sublayer transformation (attention or FFN). Across L layers, the final hidden state becomes:

h_L = h_0 + Σ_{l=0}^{L-1} F_l(...)

This is a uniform sum: every layer's contribution has equal weight. As depth increases, each individual layer's output becomes a progressively smaller fraction of the total. At L=93 layers (K3's depth), a single layer contributes ~1% of the final representation. This "depth dilution" means the model may struggle to utilize all available layers effectively — later layers' updates get lost in the accumulated sum.

The Attention Residuals paper (arXiv:2603.15031) proposes replacing fixed weights with learned attention over layer outputs, allowing the model to dynamically weight which layers matter for the current token.

4.2 Full AttnRes

Full Attention Residuals computes:

h_{l+1} = Σ_{i=0}^{l} α_i · F_i(...)

Where α_i are attention weights computed from a query derived from the current hidden state. At layer l, the model attends over all previous layers' outputs {F_0, F_1, ..., F_l} and forms a weighted combination.

This gives the model fine-grained control: it can learn that for certain tokens, early layers matter more (e.g., syntactic processing), while for others, later layers dominate (e.g., high-level reasoning). The attention mechanism adapts per-token and per-layer.

Cost: Full AttnRes requires attending over l sources at layer l, yielding O(L²) memory and compute overhead across all layers. For L=93, this is significant.

4.3 Block AttnRes (K3 Implementation)

Block AttnRes groups layers into B blocks and applies attention only at block boundaries. Within a block, standard additive residuals; across blocks, learned attention over block-level representations.

Algorithm:

  1. Partition layers into B blocks (e.g., 93 layers → ~12 blocks of ~8 layers each)
  2. Within-block: Use standard residuals h = h_prev + F(h_prev)
  3. Block summary: At the end of each block, compute a summary representation (e.g., the final hidden state of that block)
  4. Cross-block attention: At the start of each new block, attend over all previous block summaries and current hidden state

This reduces attention overhead from O(L²) to O(B²). With B=12 blocks, the overhead is ~1.7% of Full AttnRes's cost, while recovering most of the quality gains.

From the Block AttnRes paper: "With ~8 blocks, it recovers most of Full AttnRes's gains while serving as a practical drop-in replacement with marginal overhead."

4.4 Memory & Communication Overhead

Block AttnRes requires:

  • Storage: B block-level summary vectors (each of dimension d_model = 7168)
  • Computation: Attention over B+1 sources (B previous blocks + current state) at block boundaries
  • Communication (in distributed training): Block summaries must be communicated across pipeline stages

The K3 paper mentions "cache-based pipeline communication and a two-phase computation strategy" to make Block AttnRes efficient during training. Block summaries are cached and reused, avoiding redundant recomputation.

4.5 Training Dynamics

Attention Residuals change gradient flow. In standard residuals, gradients flow uniformly through all layers (the "gradient highway"). With AttnRes, gradients are modulated by the learned attention weights α_i.

This can improve training stability: if a layer is not contributing useful information, its attention weight α decreases, reducing its gradient contribution and preventing it from disrupting learning in other layers. Conversely, high-contributing layers receive stronger gradients, accelerating their learning.

Empirically, Block AttnRes enables scaling to greater depth (93 layers in K3) while maintaining quality. The ability to selectively attend to relevant layer outputs mitigates the depth dilution problem, allowing the model to make effective use of all 93 layers.

5. Stable LatentMoE

5.1 Latent Space Projection

Standard MoE architectures route tokens directly in the model's hidden dimension (d_model = 7168 for K3). This means:

  • Router computes scores over 7168-dim representations
  • Each expert processes 7168-dim inputs/outputs
  • Expert parameters scale as O(d_model²) for FFN layers

Stable LatentMoE introduces a compression step: project token representations to a latent dimension ℓ < d_model before routing and expert computation, then project back to d_model.

Architecture:

x ∈ ℝ^{d_model}  →  [Project to latent]  →  z ∈ ℝ^ℓ
z  →  [Router: select top-k experts]
z  →  [Expert computation in ℓ-dim space]  →  y ∈ ℝ^ℓ
y  →  [Project back to d_model]  →  output ∈ ℝ^{d_model}

For K3: d_model = 7168, ℓ = 3584 (exactly half). This halves the dimensionality of routing and expert computation.

5.2 Routing Mechanism

In latent space z ∈ ℝ^3584, the router computes:

scores = softmax(W_router · z)  ∈ ℝ^{896}

Where W_router ∈ ℝ^{896×3584}. The top-16 experts by score are selected (top-k routing with k=16).

Additionally, 2 shared experts are always activated for every token. These shared experts see all tokens and can learn generally useful transformations, while routed experts specialize.

Total active experts per token: 16 routed + 2 shared = 18 experts. Out of 896 routed experts, 16 are selected → 16/896 = 1.78% activation rate for routed experts.

5.3 Expert Architecture

Each expert is a standard FFN with SiTU-GLU activation:

expert(z) = W_down · SiTU-GLU(W_up(z), W_gate(z))

Dimensions:

  • Input/output: ℓ = 3584
  • Hidden dimension per expert: 3072
  • W_up, W_gate: ℝ^{3072×3584} each
  • W_down: ℝ^{3584×3072}

Parameter count per expert: (3584 × 3072) × 2 (up + gate) + (3072 × 3584) (down) ≈ 33M parameters.

Total expert parameters per layer: 896 routed × 33M + 2 shared × 33M = 29.6B + 66M ≈ 29.7B per layer. Across 93 layers: 29.7B × 93 ≈ 2.76T parameters in experts alone, matching the ~2.7T estimate from earlier.

5.4 Load Balancing & Stability

With 896 experts and top-16 selection, load balancing is critical. If the router always selects the same 16 experts, the remaining 880 never train.

Stable LatentMoE applies:

  • Load balancing loss: Penalizes routers for unevenly distributing tokens across experts
  • Expert capacity limits: Each expert processes at most C tokens per batch; overflow tokens are dropped or routed to other experts
  • Noise injection during training: Small random noise added to router logits, encouraging exploration of different expert combinations

The "Stable" in Stable LatentMoE refers to optimizations that improve training stability: careful initialization of router weights, gradient clipping on expert parameters, and tuned load balancing coefficients. The K3 paper mentions empirical design space exploration that identified an optimal expert activation range of 1.5-2.0% (K3 uses 1.78%, within this range) for balancing quality and throughput.

5.5 Memory Efficiency Analysis

Savings from latent-space routing:

  1. Router parameters: ℝ^{896×3584} instead of ℝ^{896×7168} → 2× reduction
  2. Expert weight matrices: Input/output dimensions 3584 instead of 7168 → parameters scale as (3584/7168)² ≈ 0.25× (4× reduction)
  3. Activation memory: Experts process 3584-dim tensors instead of 7168-dim → 2× reduction in activation storage during training

These savings enable scaling to 896 experts per layer while keeping active compute tractable. The compression from 7168 to 3584 is carefully chosen: large enough to preserve information (half the full dimension), small enough to realize significant savings.

The latent projection layers (W_to_latent: ℝ^{7168×3584}, W_from_latent: ℝ^{3584×7168}) add ~25M + 25M = 50M parameters per layer, a small cost relative to the 29.7B in experts.

6. Feed-Forward Networks & Activation

6.1 SiTU-GLU Activation Function

K3 uses SiTU-GLU (Sigmoid-Tanh Unit with Gated Linear Unit) activation in all expert FFNs. This is a bounded gated activation designed to prevent activation explosion in very deep networks.

Standard GLU (Gated Linear Unit) computes:

GLU(x, g) = x ⊙ σ(g)

Where ⊙ is element-wise multiplication, and σ is sigmoid. The gate g modulates the signal x.

SiTU-GLU extends this with softcapping on both branches:

SiTU-GLU(x, g) = softcap(x, β₁) ⊙ σ(softcap(g, β₂))

softcap(z, β) = β · tanh(z / β)

The softcap function bounds values to the range (-β, β). As z → ∞, tanh(z/β) → 1, so softcap(z, β) → β. This prevents unbounded activation growth.

6.2 Softcapping Mechanism

K3 uses β₁ = 4 and β₂ = 25 for the up-projection and gate branches respectively.

  • softcap(x, 4): Up-projection values bounded to (-4, 4)
  • softcap(g, 25): Gate values bounded to (-25, 25) before sigmoid

Why different β values? The gate branch undergoes sigmoid, which saturates for large inputs. Allowing larger gate values (β₂=25) gives the model more dynamic range before saturation. The up-projection branch is more sensitive to explosion (it's multiplied by the gate), so it uses a tighter bound (β₁=4).

At depth 93 with 896 experts, activation explosion is a real risk. Even small per-layer growth compounds exponentially. SiTU-GLU's bounded nature ensures activations stay in a reasonable range throughout the forward pass.

6.3 FFN Dimensions & Configuration

Each expert's FFN:

Input: z ∈ ℝ^3584 (latent space)
W_up: ℝ^{3072×3584}  →  x = W_up · z  ∈ ℝ^3072
W_gate: ℝ^{3072×3584}  →  g = W_gate · z  ∈ ℝ^3072
Activation: y = SiTU-GLU(x, g)  ∈ ℝ^3072
W_down: ℝ^{3584×3072}  →  output = W_down · y  ∈ ℝ^3584

Hidden dimension 3072 is chosen relative to latent dimension 3584: ratio of 3072/3584 ≈ 0.857. This is slightly less than 1:1 (standard transformers often use 4×d_model for FFN hidden dim), reflecting the extreme sparsity — with 18 active experts, effective FFN hidden dimension is 18 × 3072 = 55,296, which is 55,296/7168 ≈ 7.7× the model dimension, similar to standard dense models.

7. KV Cache & Memory Management

7.1 KDA Recurrent State (No KV Cache)

KDA layers do not maintain a traditional KV cache. Instead, they maintain the recurrent state S ∈ ℝ^{K×V}, which has fixed size.

For 69 KDA layers with 96 heads and approximately 64-dim keys/values per head:

Memory per KDA layer ≈ 96 heads × (64 × 64) elements × 2 bytes (FP16)
                      ≈ 96 × 4,096 × 2 ≈ 786 KB per layer
Total for 69 KDA layers ≈ 54 MB

This memory footprint is constant regardless of sequence length. At 1K tokens or 1M tokens, KDA memory remains 54 MB.

7.2 MLA KV Cache Compression

The 24 Gated MLA layers do require KV cache, as they perform full attention over all past tokens. However, MLA uses low-rank compression to reduce memory:

  • Standard attention: Stores K and V vectors for every past token, each of dimension d_model
  • MLA: Projects K and V to lower-dimensional latent representations before caching

K3's MLA configuration (inferred from similar designs like DeepSeek-V3):

  • q_lora_rank: 1536 (query low-rank dimension)
  • kv_lora_rank: 512 (key/value low-rank dimension)
  • v_head_dim: 128 (value head dimension)
  • qk_nope_head_dim: 128 (query/key non-positional dimension)
  • qk_rope_head_dim: 64 (though K3 uses NoPE, so this may be zero or repurposed)

With kv_lora_rank=512, MLA caches 512-dim latent KV representations instead of 7168-dim full representations. Compression ratio: 512/7168 ≈ 7% → 14× reduction in KV cache size per token.

7.3 NoPE: No Positional Embeddings

K3 uses NoPE (No Positional Embeddings) throughout the entire network. There are no RoPE (Rotary Position Embeddings) layers, no absolute position embeddings added to input tokens, no ALiBi (Attention with Linear Biases).

How does the model know token positions?

  1. KDA's decay dynamics: The channel-wise forget gates create position-dependent behavior. Tokens further in the past have been subjected to more decay steps, so their contribution to the recurrent state is naturally attenuated. This provides an implicit notion of recency.
  2. Periodic global attention: MLA layers can attend to all past tokens. While they don't have explicit position embeddings, the model can learn position-sensitive patterns from the data itself (e.g., syntax, discourse structure).
  3. Learned position awareness: The model's training on real text, where word order matters for meaning, induces position-sensitive representations even without explicit position encodings.

Benefits of NoPE:

  • Simplicity: No need to design or tune positional encoding schemes
  • Flexibility: No hard limits from position embeddings (e.g., RoPE can extrapolate poorly beyond trained lengths)
  • Efficiency: Eliminates rotary embedding computation (RoPE requires applying rotation matrices to every query and key)

Trade-off: Without explicit position encoding, the model relies entirely on learned mechanisms to capture order. This requires careful training and architecture design (hence the hybrid KDA+MLA stack).

7.4 Memory Footprint Analysis

Total KV cache memory at 1M tokens:

KDA layers (69): 54 MB (fixed, independent of context length)
MLA layers (24): 24 layers × 96 heads × 512 latent dim × 2 (K+V) × 1M tokens × 2 bytes (FP16)
                ≈ 24 × 96 × 512 × 2 × 1M × 2
                ≈ 4.7 TB if stored naively

Wait—4.7 TB is not tractable. How does K3 handle this?

Likely answer: Quantization and caching strategies. The model ships with MXFP8 activations, so KV cache is likely stored at 8-bit (1 byte) rather than FP16 (2 bytes), halving memory to ~2.4 TB. Additionally:

  • Selective caching: Only cache KV for MLA layers that require it; KDA layers use recurrent state
  • Offloading: Long contexts may offload older KV cache to CPU DRAM or SSD, bringing it back to GPU when needed (Mooncake serving platform, described in K3 infrastructure, uses disaggregated KV cache)
  • Recomputation: For very long contexts, recompute KV on-the-fly instead of caching everything

The infrastructure paper "Mooncake: A KVCache-centric Disaggregated Architecture for LLM Serving" (arXiv:2407.00079) describes how Moonshot AI's serving platform for Kimi leverages disaggregated storage, using underutilized CPU, DRAM, and SSD resources to cache KV states, enabling million-token contexts without requiring all KV data resident in GPU memory.

8. Gated Multi-head Latent Attention (MLA)

8.1 MLA Architecture

Gated MLA compresses keys and values to a low-rank latent space before caching, reducing memory overhead while maintaining attention quality.

Standard multi-head attention:

Q = W_q · x,  K = W_k · x,  V = W_v · x
Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax(QK^T / √d_k) V

Multi-head Latent Attention:

C_kv = W_kv_proj · x  ∈ ℝ^{kv_lora_rank}  (compress to latent)
K = W_k_latent · C_kv  ∈ ℝ^{d_k}  (expand to key space)
V = W_v_latent · C_kv  ∈ ℝ^{d_v}  (expand to value space)
[Cache C_kv instead of full K, V]

At inference: retrieve cached C_kv, expand to K/V on demand

The cached representation C_kv has dimension kv_lora_rank = 512, much smaller than the full key/value dimensions.

8.2 Low-Rank Compression

Why does this work? The insight: keys and values often lie in a lower-dimensional subspace. Not all 7168 dimensions of K and V are independently necessary for attention. Compressing to 512 dimensions captures the "essential" information, with minimal quality loss.

The compression/expansion matrices (W_kv_proj, W_k_latent, W_v_latent) are learned during training, so the model optimizes the latent subspace to preserve information relevant for attention.

8.3 Gating Mechanism

The "Gated" in Gated MLA refers to an additional gating operation applied to attention outputs:

o = Attention(Q, K, V)
o_gated = o ⊙ σ(W_gate · x)

Where σ is sigmoid, and W_gate produces per-element gates. This allows the model to dynamically modulate attention outputs, similar to how SiTU-GLU gates FFN outputs.

The gating provides another form of expressivity: even if attention focuses on the wrong tokens, the gate can suppress the resulting output. Conversely, for important tokens, the gate amplifies the attention signal.

8.4 Role in Hybrid Stack

Gated MLA layers serve as "global checkpoints" in the 3:1 hybrid stack. Every 4th layer, the model performs full global attention, allowing it to:

  • Integrate information from across the entire sequence (KDA's fixed state may have lost fine-grained details)
  • Correct errors or biases accumulated in KDA's recurrent processing
  • Provide gradient pathways that depend on long-range dependencies (important for training on long contexts)

The final layer (layer 93) is a Gated MLA layer, ensuring the model's final representation has attended globally before producing the output distribution over vocabulary.

9. Hybrid Attention Stack Design

9.1 3:1 Interleaving Pattern

K3's 93 layers follow a precise pattern:

Layers 1-3:   KDA, KDA, KDA
Layer 4:      Gated MLA
Layers 5-7:   KDA, KDA, KDA
Layer 8:      Gated MLA
...
[Repeat 23 times: 23 × 4 = 92 layers]
Layer 93:     Gated MLA (final layer)

This yields 69 KDA + 24 Gated MLA = 93 total.

9.2 Why Hybrid? Performance Trade-offs

Pure KDA (linear attention only):

  • Pro: O(1) memory per token, extremely efficient for long contexts
  • Con: Fixed recurrent state limits information capacity; quality may degrade on tasks requiring precise recall of distant details

Pure MLA (full attention only):

  • Pro: Unlimited information capacity (stores all past)
  • Con: O(n) memory and compute per token; does not scale to 1M contexts without extreme compression or approximation

3:1 Hybrid (K3's choice):

  • 74% of layers use efficient linear attention (KDA)
  • 26% of layers use global attention (MLA) for "checkpoints"
  • Balances efficiency and quality: most computation is linear-cost, but model can access full context every 4 layers

The ratio 3:1 was likely tuned empirically. Moonshot AI tested various configurations and found this balance optimal for the target compute budget and quality metrics.

9.3 Layer-by-Layer Breakdown

Simplified view of forward pass through one 4-layer block:

Input: h_0 ∈ ℝ^{7168}

Layer 1 (KDA):
  Recurrent state S_1 updated with (k_1, v_1, β_1, δ_1)
  Attention output: o_1 = query S_1 with q_1
  Apply Block AttnRes (attend over previous block summaries)
  FFN: MoE with 16/896 experts selected
  Output: h_1

Layers 2-3: Same as Layer 1, updating recurrent state S_2, S_3

Layer 4 (Gated MLA):
  Compress to latent: C_kv = W_kv_proj · h_3
  Expand to keys/values: K, V = f(C_kv)
  Full attention over all cached C_kv from previous MLA layers
  Gate attention output: o_4 = o ⊙ σ(W_gate · h_3)
  Apply Block AttnRes
  FFN: MoE
  Output: h_4

[This h_4 becomes input to next KDA block...]

The interleaving ensures no long "runs" of KDA without global attention. Maximum distance between MLA layers is 3 (when you have three consecutive KDA layers). This keeps the model grounded, preventing KDA's recurrent state from drifting too far from ground truth.

10. Multimodal Vision: MoonViT-V2

10.1 Architecture Specifications

Kimi K3 and K3.1 Pro are natively multimodal, supporting both text and image inputs. Vision is handled by MoonViT-V2, a vision transformer encoder with 401M parameters.

Key properties:

  • Native resolution: Processes images at their native resolution rather than resizing to a fixed size (e.g., 224×224)
  • Trained from scratch via next-token prediction alongside text tokens, not initialized from a pretrained vision model
  • 2D RoPE (2D Rotary Position Embeddings): Encodes spatial position in 2D image coordinates
  • Pixel-unshuffle downsampling: Uses 3×3 pixel-unshuffle to downsample images, preserving information while reducing spatial dimensions

Image tokens are interleaved with text tokens in the input sequence. The text backbone (93-layer transformer) processes both modalities uniformly after MoonViT-V2 encodes images to token embeddings.

10.2 Training Methodology

MoonViT-V2 was trained from scratch during K3's pre-training, using next-token prediction as the sole objective. This is a departure from two-stage approaches (pretrain vision encoder separately, then train adapter layers).

Benefits:

  • End-to-end optimization: Vision and language representations co-evolve, enabling tighter integration
  • No modality gap: Avoids mismatches that can occur when vision and language encoders are trained separately with different objectives
  • Unified scaling: Scaling pre-training compute improves both modalities simultaneously

The native-resolution design allows the model to handle images of varying sizes without distortion. The 3×3 pixel-unshuffle downsamples images efficiently while retaining spatial structure (unlike aggressive resizing or pooling).

10.3 Integration with Text Backbone

After MoonViT-V2 encoding, image tokens enter the same 93-layer transformer as text tokens. The model learns to process multimodal sequences, attending across both text and image tokens.

Example input sequence:

[Text tokens: "What is in this image?"]  +  [Image tokens: encoded by MoonViT-V2]  +  [Text tokens: " Answer:"]

The model generates a text response by attending to both the question text and image content. The hybrid KDA+MLA stack processes this multimodal sequence, with KDA maintaining recurrent state over both text and image tokens, and MLA layers attending globally across the full sequence.

11. Quantization: MXFP4 & MXFP8

11.1 Microscaling Floating Point Format

MXFP (Microscaling Floating Point) is a quantization format that divides tensors into small blocks (e.g., 32 elements) and assigns a shared exponent (scale factor) per block, with each element having its own mantissa and sign.

Format structure:

Block of N elements (N=32 typical):
  - Shared exponent: 8 bits
  - Per-element: sign (1 bit) + mantissa (M bits)

MXFP4: M=2 (sign + 2-bit mantissa per element + 8-bit shared exponent per block)
MXFP8: M=6 (sign + 6-bit mantissa per element + 8-bit shared exponent per block)

The shared exponent enables block-level scaling, providing higher precision than fixed-point quantization (which uses one global scale) while using fewer bits than per-element FP8 (which has 8 bits per element with individual exponents).

11.2 MXFP4 for Weights

K3 ships with weights quantized to MXFP4:

  • 2-bit mantissa + 1-bit sign per weight
  • 8-bit shared exponent per 32-weight block
  • Effective: ~4.25 bits per weight (accounting for shared exponent overhead)

At 2.8T parameters, this reduces storage from ~5.6 TB (FP16) to ~1.5 TB (MXFP4), a 3.7× reduction. The actual download size for K3 weights is approximately 1.44 TB, matching this estimate.

Inference: MXFP4 weights are dequantized to higher precision (likely FP16 or BF16) before matrix multiplication, or specialized kernels perform low-bit matrix multiplication directly.

11.3 MXFP8 for Activations

During inference, activations are computed in MXFP8:

  • 6-bit mantissa + 1-bit sign per activation
  • 8-bit shared exponent per block
  • Provides higher precision than MXFP4 (activations need more dynamic range than weights)

This includes:

  • Hidden states h ∈ ℝ^{7168}
  • Attention outputs, FFN intermediate activations
  • KV cache (cached in MXFP8 instead of FP16 → 2× memory reduction)
  • KDA recurrent state S

11.4 Quality Impact Analysis

Quantization introduces approximation error. For MXFP4 weights, each weight is represented by only 4 possible mantissa values (2 bits) × 2 signs = 8 discrete levels per block scale. This is extremely aggressive quantization.

Surprisingly, K3/K3.1 Pro maintains high quality despite MXFP4 weights. Likely reasons:

  1. Quantization-aware training or post-training quantization with careful calibration
  2. Large model capacity: 2.8T parameters provides redundancy; quantization error in individual weights is averaged out
  3. MoE sparsity: Only 16/896 experts active, so the model effectively has many parallel pathways; if one expert suffers from quantization error, others can compensate
  4. Microscaling benefits: Block-level exponents adapt to local scale, preventing extreme quantization error

Independent benchmarks (Artificial Analysis, Terminal-Bench) show K3.1 Pro at MXFP4/MXFP8 achieves competitive scores against full-precision proprietary models, suggesting quantization degradation is minimal.

13. Training Methodology

13.1 Pre-Training (K3 Base)

K3's pre-training followed standard large-scale language model practices: next-token prediction on a diverse web-scale corpus. The K3 technical paper states the model achieved "approximately 2.5× improvement in overall scaling efficiency over Kimi K2," attributing this to KDA, AttnRes, Stable LatentMoE, and refined data recipes.

Pre-training objectives:

  • Text: Standard causal language modeling (predict next token given context)
  • Vision: MoonViT-V2 trained end-to-end via next-token prediction on interleaved text-image sequences

Infrastructure: The paper mentions "perfectly balanced expert-parallel training with efficient memory management" as a key enabler. With 896 experts per layer across 93 layers (83,328 total expert instances), naive data parallelism would cause severe load imbalance. Moonshot's expert-parallel system ensures all experts receive equal numbers of tokens during training, preventing underutilization.

13.2 Mid-Training & Data Mix

After pre-training, K3 underwent mid-training on a curated mix emphasizing longer-context and domain-specific data. This phase bridges pre-training (broad, noisy web data) and post-training (task-specific RL).

For K3.1 Pro, Moonshot applied cyber-related data removal during mid-training. Content flagged as cyber-related (exploit write-ups, vulnerability databases, offensive security tutorials) was identified and removed from the corpus. The model was then continued from the pre-trained K3 checkpoint with the cleaned mid-training data.

This approach allows control without full retraining: start from K3's pre-trained weights, apply a focused mid-training phase with removed data, then proceed to RL post-training.

13.3 Reinforcement Learning Pipeline

K3.1 Pro's RL training uses multi-effort setups: the model is trained to operate at different "reasoning effort" levels (low, medium, high, max), allowing users to trade latency for quality.

RL Algorithm (likely PPO or similar policy gradient method):

  1. Rollout: Generate completions for prompts from RL environments (code generation tasks, math problems, agentic workflows)
  2. Reward computation: Execute generated code in sandbox, verify math answers, check agentic task success; assign scalar rewards
  3. Advantage estimation: Compare actual returns vs. value function estimates to determine which actions were better than expected
  4. Policy update: Adjust model weights to increase probability of high-reward actions

Joint RL across hundreds of domains means the rollout phase samples prompts from diverse environments (coding, 3D modeling, financial analysis, etc.) within each training batch, and the policy update considers all domains simultaneously.

13.4 Multi-Effort RL Training

K3 introduced "reasoning-effort levels" during RL training. The model learns to allocate compute based on a user-specified effort parameter:

  • Low effort: Fast, shorter responses, suitable for simple queries
  • Medium effort: Balanced
  • High effort: More thorough reasoning, longer responses
  • Max effort: Extensive deliberation, multiple verification passes

Training involves conditioning the policy on effort level: prompts are tagged with an effort indicator, and rewards are adjusted to encourage effort-appropriate behavior (e.g., penalize overly long responses at low effort, reward thoroughness at max effort).

This enables a single model to serve multiple use cases without fine-tuning separate variants.

13.5 Long-Context Agentic RL

The K3 technical paper highlights "million-token agentic RL with persistent rollout and sandbox states." This is a technical challenge: RL episodes can involve million-token contexts (large codebases, long documents), and sandboxes (execution environments) must persist across multi-turn interactions.

Innovations:

  • Persistent sandbox states: When the agent writes code, modifies files, or installs dependencies, these changes persist across turns within the same episode. The agent can build up complex state (e.g., a working web server) over multiple actions.
  • Efficient long-context inference: KDA's O(1) memory and MLA's compressed KV cache enable generating responses for million-token prompts within reasonable latency. During RL rollout, every sampled action requires inference on the full context; slow inference would bottleneck training.
  • Rollout scheduling: Load balancer distributes episodes across sandboxes to maximize GPU utilization. If one episode is waiting for code execution (CPU-bound), the GPU works on other episodes.

K3.1 Pro's infrastructure optimizations (§12.4) directly target these challenges, enabling stable RL training at million-token scale.

13.6 Sandbox Infrastructure

Sandboxes are isolated Linux containers (likely Docker or similar) where generated code executes safely. Each sandbox has:

  • Standard development tools (compilers, interpreters, package managers)
  • Network access (controlled, for web search and API calls)
  • File system (persistent within an episode, wiped between episodes)
  • Resource limits (CPU, memory, time) to prevent runaway processes

Automated verification: After code execution, an automated grader checks whether the task was completed correctly (e.g., does the program output match the expected result? Did the agent successfully fix the bug?). This provides the reward signal for RL.

Quality checks: The "shared platform for environment quality checks" (mentioned by Moonshot) ensures sandboxes are set up correctly — dependencies installed, necessary files present, network accessible. Silent failures (e.g., sandbox missing a required library, causing all code to fail) would poison the training signal; automated checks catch these issues.

16. Deployment & Infrastructure

16.1 Hardware Requirements

K3/K3.1 Pro at 2.8T parameters and MXFP4 quantization requires approximately 1.5 TB storage. For inference:

  • Moonshot's recommendation: Supernodes of 64+ accelerators (GPUs or NPUs)
  • Memory per accelerator: 1.5 TB / 64 ≈ 23 GB per device (feasible with 80GB A100s or H100s)
  • Active parameters: 104B active per token is ~208 GB at FP16, ~104 GB at MXFP8, distributed across devices

For smaller deployments: With aggressive quantization and expert offloading, running on fewer devices is possible but will increase latency. The 896 experts per layer create natural parallelism — different devices can host different experts.

16.2 Serving Infrastructure

Moonshot AI's Mooncake serving platform (arXiv:2407.00079) is designed for K3-class models. Key features:

  • Disaggregated KV cache: KV cache stored separately from compute, using CPU DRAM and SSD in addition to GPU memory
  • Prefill/decode separation: Separate clusters for prefill (processing prompts) and decode (generating tokens), optimizing resource utilization
  • Expert caching: Frequently-used experts cached on-device; less-used experts loaded on-demand

This architecture enables serving million-token contexts economically. The KDA layers' fixed recurrent state (no KV cache) reduces memory pressure significantly.

16.3 KV Cache Management at Scale

At 1M tokens with 24 MLA layers:

  • Latent KV cache: 24 layers × 512 latent dim × 2 (K+V) × 1M tokens ≈ 24M elements
  • At MXFP8: ~24 MB per sequence (ignoring shared exponent overhead)
  • Batch size 100: ~2.4 GB for all KV cache (tractable on GPU)

Wait—this seems too small. The 512 latent dim must be per-head, not total. With 96 heads:

  • 24 layers × 96 heads × 512 × 2 × 1M × 1 byte (MXFP8) ≈ 2.4 TB per sequence

This matches the earlier estimate. At this scale, KV cache cannot fit in GPU memory for batches > 1. Solutions:

  1. Offload to CPU/SSD: Store older KV in CPU DRAM or SSD, page in as needed
  2. Streaming generation: Single-sequence inference (batch size 1), acceptable for interactive use
  3. Selective caching: Only cache KV for recent tokens; recompute older KV on-demand

Mooncake's disaggregated architecture addresses this: KV cache lives in a distributed store accessed over fast interconnect, not on the GPU.

16.4 Expert Parallelism

With 896 experts per layer, expert parallelism is essential. Distribution strategies:

  • Expert sharding: Each device hosts a subset of experts (e.g., device 0 hosts experts 0-13, device 1 hosts 14-27, etc.)
  • All-to-all communication: Tokens routed to experts on different devices require all-to-all communication to gather inputs and scatter outputs
  • Load balancing: Ensure expert selection is balanced; if expert 0 gets 10× more tokens than expert 100, device hosting expert 0 becomes a bottleneck

K3's Stable LatentMoE includes load balancing losses to encourage uniform expert utilization, mitigating this issue.

17. Limitations & Considerations

17.1 Scientific Reasoning Gap

K3.1 Pro's most significant weakness is scientific reasoning. Terminal-Bench Science 0.1 (38.1%) trails GPT-6 Astra (68.1%) and Claude Opus 5.5 (65.5%) by ~30 points. This gap persists across other scientific benchmarks (physics, chemistry, advanced mathematics).

Possible causes:

  • Training data composition: K3's pre-training mix may underweight scientific content
  • RL environment coverage: K3.1 Pro's RL training emphasizes coding, agentic, and knowledge work; scientific domains may be underrepresented
  • Architectural constraints: KDA's fixed recurrent state may struggle with complex multi-step mathematical reasoning requiring precise intermediate results

For use cases requiring strong scientific reasoning, proprietary alternatives (Claude Opus, GPT-6 Astra) remain superior.

17.2 Compute Requirements

Despite efficiency innovations (KDA, MoE sparsity), K3.1 Pro remains a 2.8T-parameter model requiring substantial hardware:

  • Storage: 1.5 TB for weights
  • Inference: Recommended 64+ GPUs/NPUs, realistically 80GB A100/H100 class
  • Long-context: Million-token inference requires disaggregated KV cache infrastructure

This is not a model for consumer hardware or small-scale deployment. Practitioners without access to large GPU clusters will need to use hosted APIs (Kimi API Platform) rather than self-hosting.

17.3 Known Failure Modes

From Moonshot's evaluations and independent testing:

  • Hallucination on stress tests: Despite 65% reduction (23.4% → 8.3%), K3.1 Pro still produces factual errors on deliberately challenging prompts
  • Instruction following edge cases: Complex multi-constraint instructions (e.g., "write a poem about X without using letter E, in iambic pentameter, with Y theme") may fail to satisfy all constraints
  • Long-context degradation: While K3.1 Pro supports 1M tokens, quality may degrade for queries requiring precise recall from early context (the KDA fixed-state bottleneck)

19. Conclusion

Kimi K3.1 Pro represents a milestone in open-weight model development: a 2.8T-parameter system with 104B active parameters, ranking 4th globally on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index and 3rd on GDPval-AA, demonstrating that open weights can compete with proprietary frontier models.

The underlying K3 architecture introduces three fundamental innovations — Kimi Delta Attention (linear attention with channel-wise gating), Block Attention Residuals (learned depth-wise weighting), and Stable LatentMoE (latent-space routing with 896 experts per layer) — that enable efficient scaling to 2.8T parameters while maintaining 104B active compute per token. The hybrid 3:1 KDA:MLA attention stack balances efficiency (74% linear attention) with modeling capacity (26% global attention), and NoPE (No Positional Embeddings) simplifies the architecture while enabling flexible long-context processing.

K3.1 Pro's improvements over K3 stem entirely from substantially scaled post-training reinforcement learning across hundreds of domains, with no architectural changes. The 11-point Intelligence Index gain (44 → 55) and 312-Elo GDPval-AA improvement (1,534 → 1,846) demonstrate that post-training RL, when applied with optimized infrastructure (joint multi-domain training, efficient long-context inference, persistent sandbox states, reliable environment quality checks), can produce gains comparable to historical pre-training scaling.

Key results:

  • Terminal-Bench 4.0: 65.7%, ahead of GPT-6 Astra (58.2%), narrowly behind Claude Opus 5.5 (66.4%)
  • SWE-Marathon: 51.7%, highest among compared models
  • GDPval-AA: 1,846 Elo, 3rd globally
  • Hallucination reduction: 65% (23.4% → 8.3% on stress tests)
  • Intelligence Index: 55 (4th globally), 3-point gap from 1st place

Limitations remain: scientific reasoning trails frontier models by ~30 points (Terminal-Bench Science 38.1% vs. GPT-6 Astra 68.1%), and deployment requires substantial hardware (64+ GPUs recommended). Cybersecurity controls (data removal) reduced xdev-bench-hard from 90.5% to 81.0%, demonstrating feasible paths for managing dual-use capabilities in open releases.

With full weights scheduled for November 4, 2026 under Apache 2.0 license, K3.1 Pro will provide the research community and practitioners with frontier-level capabilities at 2.8T scale, the largest open-weight model available. The architecture's efficiency innovations (KDA's O(1) memory, MoE sparsity, MXFP4 quantization) make deployment tractable, and the post-training methodology (joint RL across domains, multi-effort training) establishes a template for future open-weight development.

K3.1 Pro demonstrates that the open-weight frontier can reach competitive parity with proprietary systems through architectural efficiency and scaled post-training, narrowing the capability gap that has historically separated open and closed models.
