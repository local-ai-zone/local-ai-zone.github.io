Meta Muse Wonder & Wonder Full: A Complete Technical Deep Dive

On October 12, 2026, Meta Superintelligence Labs released Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full—Meta's first true frontier AI models. Trained at the gigawatt-scale Prometheus data center, these models represent Meta's return to the top tier of AI development, just 14 months after a comprehensive reorganization. This deep dive examines everything Meta has publicly shared about the architecture, training, capabilities, and positioning of the Muse Wonder family.

What Is Meta Muse Wonder?

Meta Muse Wonder is a frontier-class large language model family designed specifically for personal and professional agentic tasks. Released on October 12, 2026, the family includes two distinct variants:

  • Muse Wonder — The standard variant, available through the Meta Model API at $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens
  • Muse Wonder Full — The more capable variant, exclusive to Muse Code subscribers on the $50 Power Usage and $200 Maximum Usage plans

Both models support a 1,048,576-token context window and accept multimodal inputs (text, images, audio, video, and PDF files) while outputting text only. They are the first models from Meta Superintelligence Labs (MSL), the division formed after Meta's August 2025 AI reorganization under Chief AI Officer Alexandr Wang.

"Trained at our gigawatt-scale Prometheus data center, Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full bring together the work of teams across Meta to deliver frontier capabilities for complex tasks at work and in everyday life—another step toward bringing personal superintelligence to the billions of people who use our products."
— Meta AI Research Blog

Architecture and Scale

Parameter Count: ~3 Trillion Total

While Meta has not officially disclosed the exact parameter count, industry sources estimate Muse Wonder has approximately 3 trillion total parameters. According to industry analysis, channel checks indicate Wonder is smaller than both GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.1, but still firmly in the frontier model category.

"Our channel checks indicate that Wonder has roughly 3T total parameters, making it smaller than both Astra and Fable."
— Industry Analysis Report, October 2026

This places Muse Wonder in a competitive position:

  • Smaller than: GPT-6 Astra (~5-6T estimated), Claude Fable 5.1 (~4-5T estimated)
  • Larger than: Mistral Large 4 (1T total, 49B active), most open-weight models
  • Comparable to: Mid-tier frontier models from 2026

Prometheus Data Center: Gigawatt-Scale Infrastructure

Both Muse Wonder variants were trained at Meta's Prometheus data center, described as a "gigawatt-scale" facility located in central Ohio. This represents one of the largest AI training installations in the world.

Key infrastructure details:

  • Power scale: Gigawatt-class (1,000+ megawatts)
  • Location: Central Ohio, USA
  • Purpose-built: Designed specifically for frontier AI training workloads
  • Timeline: Operational since late 2025, with ongoing expansions

Training Methodology: From Spark to Wonder

Meta's approach to developing Wonder followed a deliberate, multi-generation strategy:

  1. Recipe Validation: The team validated training recipes, data mixtures, and RL environments on small-scale models
  2. Incremental Scaling: Each generation increased model size by 4-5x
  3. Spark Foundation: Muse Spark (codenamed "Avocado") served as the foundation, with pretraining beginning in late 2025
  4. Wonder Training (codenamed "Watermelon"): Planning began in August 2025, culminating in the October 2026 release

This represents faster iteration than Meta achieved during the Llama era. The transition from planning to frontier-competitive model took just over 14 months—a remarkable pace in frontier AI development.

Multimodal Architecture

Muse Wonder is designed as a natively multimodal system, accepting:

  • Text: Natural language prompts, code, structured data
  • Images: Photos, diagrams, screenshots, charts
  • Audio: Voice recordings, music, sound effects
  • Video: Short clips, demonstrations, screen recordings
  • PDF Files: Documents with mixed text and images

However, the model outputs text only. For image and video generation, Meta offers separate products (Muse Image and Muse Video).

What Meta Has Not Disclosed: Architecture Specifications

Meta has kept the detailed architecture specifications of Muse Wonder proprietary. Unlike open-weight models where technical details are published, the following information remains undisclosed:

  • Number of transformer layers
  • Attention heads per layer
  • Hidden dimension size
  • Feedforward network dimensions
  • Mixture-of-Experts configuration (if applicable)
  • Specific attention mechanisms (e.g., grouped-query attention, sliding window)
  • Vocabulary size and tokenizer details
  • Position encoding method (RoPE, ALiBi, etc.)
  • Training data composition and size
  • Reinforcement learning techniques used
  • Exact pretraining and post-training procedures

This is a deliberate strategic choice by Meta. While the company open-sources its Llama model family with full technical specifications, Muse Wonder and Wonder Full are positioned as proprietary commercial models competing directly with OpenAI's GPT series and Anthropic's Claude family.

Why this matters: Without architecture details, researchers cannot reproduce the model, fine-tune it for specialized tasks, or run it on custom infrastructure. The only way to use Muse Wonder is through Meta's controlled API or Muse Code subscription, giving Meta complete control over pricing, access policies, and deployment.

The limited public information consists of:

  • Approximate parameter count: ~3T (from industry analysis, not official disclosure)
  • Training infrastructure: Prometheus data center, gigawatt-scale
  • Training timeline: Planning began August 2025, released October 2026
  • Scaling methodology: 4-5x parameter increase per generation
  • High-level capabilities: Active Memory, Active Focus, multimodal inputs
  • Context window: 1,048,576 tokens
  • Performance benchmarks: Independently evaluated by Artificial Analysis

This contrasts sharply with models like Qwen, Mistral, and Llama, where detailed architecture specifications, model weights, and technical papers are publicly available. Even closed models from competitors like Anthropic occasionally publish architectural insights in research papers.

Meta's approach with Muse Wonder mirrors OpenAI's GPT-4 strategy: share high-level capabilities and benchmark results, but keep the architectural recipe proprietary to maintain competitive advantage.

Core Capabilities: Active Memory and Active Focus

Meta describes two flagship capabilities that define the Muse Wonder family: Active Memory and Active Focus. These represent specific architectural and training decisions that differentiate Wonder from conventional chatbots.

Active Memory: Context That Persists

Active Memory is Meta's term for the model's ability to:

  • Retain relevant information across conversations spanning days, weeks, or months
  • Retrieve context when it becomes relevant to a new task
  • Recognize patterns in user preferences, constraints, and goals

Traditional conversational AI loses important details as tasks grow longer. Active Memory addresses this by training the model to maintain and surface relevant context at the right moments.

Example scenario: An AI travel agent needs to combine a user's dietary preferences (mentioned two weeks ago) with a new calendar event, transportation constraints, and updated destination information—all while respecting a previously stated budget.

On Meta's internal MemoryBench evaluation:

  • Muse Wonder: 85.6
  • Muse Wonder Full: 85.5
  • Claude Opus 5.5: ~81.5
  • GPT-6 Astra: ~81.5

Active Focus: Persistent Execution Across Complex Tasks

Active Focus describes Wonder's ability to:

  • Sustain effort on difficult objectives over multiple steps
  • Try alternative approaches when initial attempts fail
  • Recover from errors in tool execution or API failures
  • Ask for clarification when needed rather than guessing
  • Confirm important actions before execution

This capability is especially critical for long-running AI agents. Consider an autonomous coding task:

  1. Inspect repository structure
  2. Identify dependencies and constraints
  3. Modify multiple files
  4. Run test suite
  5. Diagnose test failures
  6. Revise implementation
  7. Re-test until passing

A model that produces convincing initial code but can't respond to failed tests has limited practical value. Active Focus trains Wonder to persist through blockers instead of stopping and handing the task back to the user.

How to Use Muse Wonder: Developer Guide

Step 1: Obtain API Access

  1. Visit the Meta Developer Platform
  2. Create an API key through the supported account workflow
  3. Store the key securely as an environment variable (MODEL_API_KEY)

Step 2: Install the OpenAI-Compatible SDK

The Meta Model API supports OpenAI-compatible SDK integrations. For Node.js/TypeScript:

npm install openai

Step 3: Make Your First Request

import OpenAI from 'openai';

const client = new OpenAI({
  baseURL: 'https://api.meta.ai/v1',
  apiKey: process.env.MODEL_API_KEY,
});

async function callMuseWonder() {
  const response = await client.chat.completions.create({
    model: 'muse-wonder-1.0',
    messages: [
      {
        role: 'user',
        content: 'Explain Active Memory in Muse Wonder.'
      }
    ],
    max_tokens: 1000,
  });

  console.log(response.choices[0].message.content);
}

callMuseWonder();

Best Practices for Building Agents with Muse Wonder

1. Define Verifiable Acceptance Criteria

Instead of vague goals like "improve the application," specify:

  • Relevant files to modify
  • Expected behavior after changes
  • Tests that must pass
  • Completion criteria

2. Separate Planning and Execution

For consequential operations (deployments, destructive changes, financial transactions), require an implementation plan before execution. Human approval checkpoints reduce the risk of completing the wrong task.

3. Use Prompt Caching for Repeated Context

Large repositories and multi-agent workflows often reuse substantial context. Caching repeated prompt prefixes can reduce costs by 90% on cached portions.

4. Route by Task Complexity

Not every request justifies a premium frontier model:

  • Muse Spark → Classification, extraction, summarization
  • Muse Wonder → Complex planning, multi-step workflows
  • Muse Wonder Full → High-difficulty engineering work

5. Use Persistent Checkpoints

Long-running agents should store task progress outside the prompt:

  • Completed subtasks
  • Modified files
  • Test reports
  • Outstanding issues

Privacy and Data Usage

Meta Model API: Standard Mode

According to Meta's published policies, Standard mode does not use submitted prompts, code, files, context, or model responses to train future Meta models. Regular Muse Wonder is available through the public API in Standard mode only.

Muse Code: Contributor Mode

Muse Code subscriptions default to Contributor mode, which allows Meta to use submitted content (including code and model responses) for future model training. Subscribers can turn Contributor mode off in settings.

Important for enterprise users: The distinction matters for proprietary repositories, customer data, internal documents, and confidential business processes.
